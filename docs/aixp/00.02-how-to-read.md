# How to Read This Book

The practices in this book form a system. Individual practices reinforce each other in ways that only become obvious once several are running simultaneously - TDD without Spec-First Prompting is catching the wrong failures; Executive Code Review without Agent Role Topology produces feedback with no clear owner; Vigilance Budgeting without Context Stewardship means the session limits help but the context loss compounds.

That said, you don't need to adopt everything at once. The structure is:

**Part I - Background** sets the analytical foundation: what's wrong with how most people use AI tools today, what Extreme Programming's original insight was and why it transfers, and what specifically changed about the economics of software development when AI entered the workflow.

**Part II - The Manifesto** is a short statement of pillars and rejection criteria - what aiXP is committed to and what it explicitly rejects. Read this before the practices if you want the frame first; read it after if you'd rather ground the abstraction in concrete examples.

**Part III - The Practices** is the core of the book: fifteen practices organized into three groups (XP-derived, aiXP-native, DevOps/SRE-adopted) plus two primers on testing strategy and versioning discipline. This is the reference material you'll come back to.

**Part IV - Context and Comparison** situates aiXP relative to the broader "AI-native SDLC" conversation, addresses what happens when you scale beyond a solo practitioner, and catalogs the common failure modes - what aiXP wrong looks like.

**If you're coming from XP:** start with Part III's first chapter (the practice-by-practice comparison table) and work forward. The modifications to familiar practices will orient you to what's genuinely new.

**If you're coming in fresh:** start with Part I, then the Manifesto, then the practices in order. The testing and versioning primers (Parts 4 and 5) are intentionally written as introductions, not refreshers.

**If you want the short version:** read the Manifesto and Practice 7 (Vigilance Budgeting). If those make sense and feel important, the rest follows.
