## How to Read This Book

The practices in this book form a system. Individual practices reinforce each other in ways that only become obvious once several are running simultaneously - TDD without Spec-First Prompting is catching the wrong failures; Executive Code Review without Agent Role Topology produces feedback with no clear owner; Vigilance Budgeting without Context Stewardship means session limits help but context loss compounds.

That said, you do not need to adopt everything at once. The book is organized around the engineer's active workflow across nine sequential chapters:

- **The Introduction** sets the analytical foundation: diagnosing what goes wrong with "vibe coding", what Extreme Programming's original bet was and why it transfers, and how the economics of software inverted when AI entered the workflow.
- **Chapter 1: The Manifesto** is a short statement of pillars and operational rejection criteria - what aiXP is committed to and what it explicitly rejects. Read this first if you want the philosophical compass; read it after the practices if you prefer to ground abstractions in concrete examples.
- **Chapters 2 through 6: The Practice Core** follow the engineer's daily workflow from intent to production:
  - *Chapter 2 (Intent and Context):* Grounding specifications, surfacing domain gaps, and establishing context infrastructure before writing code.
  - *Chapter 3 (The Terminal Loop):* Asymmetric pairing, Test-Driven Development (TDD), bounded refactoring, and simple design at the keyboard.
  - *Chapter 4 (Judgment and Roles):* Structuring agent roles, conducting executive code reviews, and budgeting human vigilance.
  - *Chapter 5 (Verification Strategy):* The four testing tiers, risk-tiered continuous integration, and machine-speed regression suites.
  - *Chapter 6 (Delivery and Observability):* Small releases, semantic release identity, session traceability, and deployment gating.
- **Chapters 7 through 9: Context, Scale, and Boundaries** situate aiXP in the broader industry landscape:
  - *Chapter 7 (Scaling):* What changes when expanding from a solo practitioner to human+agent teams.
  - *Chapter 8 (Modern Landscape):* How aiXP compares against emerging enterprise frameworks (such as Anthropic's AI-Native SDLC).
  - *Chapter 9 (What aiXP Is Not):* A catalog of common failure modes and anti-patterns.

### Reading Tracks

- **If you are an XP veteran:** Start with the Introduction and Chapter 1 (The Manifesto), then proceed straight to Chapters 2 through 4 to see how familiar practices (pairing, TDD, refactoring, sustainable pace) are re-anchored for asymmetric AI collaboration.
- **If you are coming in fresh:** Read sequentially from the Introduction through Chapter 6. Chapters 5 and 6 (Testing Strategy and Versioning) are written as pragmatic, accessible primers rather than abstract academic specifications.
- **If you want the short version:** Read Chapter 1 (The Manifesto) and Section 3 of Chapter 4 (Vigilance Budgeting). If those two make sense and feel urgent, the rest of the discipline follows naturally.
